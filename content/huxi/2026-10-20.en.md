## Ministry Fellowship

### The measure of the stature of Christ is a matter of the Body

His spiritual life was entirely in the Body, and all his spiritual experience was also in the Body. Therefore, his attaining to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ was an experience in the Body.

A person cannot experience the measure of the stature of Christ outside the Body. In fact, even in the Body he cannot attain this measure by himself. The measure of the stature of Christ is a matter of the Body. Therefore, only the Body can attain it.

The Bible mentions a full-grown man only once, in Ephesians 4:13. This does not refer to the saints individually but to the Body of Christ, the church, which will one day reach this condition. Ephesians 3:18 also says that we apprehend the breadth, length, height, and depth of Christ together with all the saints. These two passages show us that neither the measure of Christ’s stature nor His unsearchable fullness can be experienced by one person alone. We experience them together with the saints as we are joined in the Body. (*The Experience of Life*, message 19)

### The saints are built together by growing together

The saints’ being built together depends on their advancing and growing together. This building grows out of life and continues to grow. Your spirit and the spirits of the saints are blended and interwoven as one in the Holy Spirit; this is the building of the church. When everyone’s spirit comes forth and is joined as one in the Spirit of God, we can touch God’s feeling together in fellowship. (*The Priesthood and God’s Building*, message 1)

### The Spirit and our spirit: a corporate spirit, today’s Jerusalem

The phrase “in spirit” can be found in every chapter of Ephesians except the first. What is in our spirit? You may say that the Lord Jesus and the Spirit of God are in our spirit. That is certainly right. But now we must see that the new man is also in our spirit. God’s dwelling place, His abode, is in our spirit (2:22). Our problem is that we are too individualistic and think only of our own spirit. Yet whenever the Bible says “your spirit,” it includes the spirits of all the saints. We know that God dwells in the third heaven, but He also dwells in our spirit. This makes our spirit today’s Jerusalem. Hallelujah! There is a marvelous entity in the universe called our spirit. The Spirit witnesses with our spirit (Rom. 8:16). “Our spirit” includes the spirits of Paul, Martin Luther, John Wesley, Watchman Nee, you, and me. How broad our spirit is! (*Life-study of Ephesians*, message 24)

### To enter into my spirit is to enter into “our spirit”; the Body is in “our spirit”

We may think that I am in my spirit and you are in your spirit, that we are separate units in our own spirits. But those who have been dealt with, broken, reconstituted, and built realize that when they enter into their spirit, they enter into “our spirit.” The Body is in “our spirit,” and this Body transcends space and time. The spirits of Paul, John, Watchman Nee, and Brother Lee are here. (*Crystallization-study of the Building of God*, message 9)

The genuine building is not merely a matter of everyone getting along; that is only on the surface and is not deep enough. The genuine building requires everyone to be blended in one spirit and to act in one spirit. This is the real meaning of not being spiritual individually: your spirit can be blended with another’s spirit.

### In Acts 13 the spirits of five were blended as one in the Spirit of God

Acts 13:1-2 says, “Now there were in Antioch, in the local church, prophets and teachers: Barnabas and Simeon, who was called Niger, and Lucius the Cyrenian, and Manaen, the foster brother of Herod the tetrarch, and Saul. And as they were ministering to the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit said, Set apart for Me now Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them.” Here we see the five prophets and teachers in Antioch serving God with one accord. Their ministry did not refer to doing some work or carrying out some activity; it referred to their fasting and praying there. In their ministry, I believe each one’s spirit came forth, and the spirits of the five were blended as one in the Spirit of God. (*The Blueprint and the Ground for the Building Up of the Church*, message 6)

### Practice the vital groups to enliven people and bring in the genuine building

Most people throughout the earth are asleep today. People are asleep and cannot be awakened. We can become vital only by turning our church life into vital groups. (*Shepherding the Church through the Practice of the Vital Groups*, ch. 1)

To move from an ordinary or merely apparent building to the genuine building, we must practice the vital groups.

Those who serve need to realize that their inward work is to enliven the saints one by one. If a full-time serving one can enliven two people in this way, these three believers can have intimate fellowship and be built together. Without the vital groups, we may have only an ordinary building. If we would have the genuine building among us, we must practice the vital groups. Without the vital groups, the church life in a locality may largely be a performance, lacking the genuine building. I also urge the saints in every church to establish many vital groups in their locality. The more vital groups a church has, the more living that church will be. (*Collected Works of Witness Lee, 1993, vol. 1*, ch. 7)

### Become the corporate Holy of Holies, the corporate Zion, the New Jerusalem

Whenever we shepherd people, we need to reconcile them to God and reconcile them into God. Our goal is to make them persons in spirit, persons in the Holy of Holies. The Holy of Holies is the reality of Zion, and Zion is the overcomers. Eventually, through the ministry of reconciliation—the ministry in which we participate—we will become the corporate Holy of Holies, the corporate Zion, the New Jerusalem. (2007 Memorial Day Conference, message 6 transcript)

### Enter into the current of the Body of Christ

The day of the Lord’s return is near. Since His return is so near, we should not waste time seeking spirituality individually. Rather, we should enter into the current of the Body of Christ; it is worthwhile to enter into this current. When we enter into the Lord’s current, we gain Christ as everything. Everything depends on His current. We should not worry about our future, because it is in the Lord’s hand. If we enter into His current, He will take care of our future. (*The Lord’s Recovery of the Church Life*, ch. 6)
