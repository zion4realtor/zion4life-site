## Today’s Prayer Burden

An open vessel is not inactive; it acts without acting independently of the Lord. It is not merely filled with knowledge; it allows the truth to become the Christ we experience and live out. Lord, gain us as genuine open vessels, keeping us from turning aside!

## Today’s Prayer

### 1. Lord, gain us as genuine open vessels, keeping us from turning aside

Lord, save us from having knowledge without life, and speaking without living out what we speak! We do not want merely to know the Bible and the ministry without being filled with You and constituted with You. Lord, may we open to You every day, allowing You to enter, possess, saturate, and transform us. May we not only know You but experience You; not only enjoy You but live You out; not only speak of You in the meetings but express You and supply You in our daily living. Lord, gain us not as storehouses of knowledge but as vessels containing and flowing out the living Christ!

### 2. Lord, gain us as vessels one with You in Your move and continually multiplying

Lord, we do not want only to receive without flowing out, enjoy without bearing fruit, understand without practicing, or wait without acting! Make us, like Paul, vessels unto honor, useful to You, the Master. Make us teachers who pass on the truth, soldiers who fight faithfully, runners who press on, farmers who labor patiently, and workmen who cut straight the word of the Lord. Lord, send us! May we announce Your word in season and out of season, beget, nourish, teach, build, spread, and shepherd. Save us from initiating things by ourselves; keep us closely following the Spirit and fully one with Your present move!

### 3. Lord, raise up corporate open vessels to build up Your Body

Lord, we do not want to have only individual spirituality, enjoyment, or function. As You did in Antioch, gain a company of people who open to You together, serve together, and receive the Spirit’s sending together. Blend us together, break our natural disposition, and remove our independent actions. May we pray, live, exercise, spread, and shepherd together. Lord, gain not only one Paul; raise up companies of corporate Pauls! Do not merely reproduce an individual’s ability; reproduce the way of life in which all the members function together. May the vital groups continually multiply, the Body be built up, and the bride be made ready soon to welcome Your return!

## Ministry Fellowship

### An open vessel: Let the truth become the Christ we experience and live out

From Paul’s fourteen Epistles and Message Seven of the 2026 Summer Training, we see a fully equipped vessel that contains Christ, multiplies Christ, moves with the Lord, shepherds people, and builds up the Body.

#### The central burden: Paul’s fourteen Epistles can be summed up in one expression—an open vessel

In *The Perfecting Training*, Message 22, “An Open Vessel to the Lord,” Brother Lee says, “The fourteen Epistles can be summed up in one phrase: ‘an open vessel.’” The natural difficulty of man is that he likes to know and do things by himself. Even those who love the Lord may be open to their own knowledge and work without being genuinely open to the Lord. Message Seven of the 2026 Summer Training emphasizes that the extent to which God can dispense Himself into us depends on the extent to which we are open.

#### What does it mean to have knowledge without being genuinely open to the Lord?

There is nothing wrong with knowing the Bible and the ministry. But when knowledge does not become our experience of life, several conditions may result:

- Knowing much but contacting the Lord little: We can analyze the Scriptures and quote the ministry, yet have little living prayer, speaking from the Lord, or supply in our spirit (John 5:39–40).
- Speaking much but living out little: We can speak of Christ as life, yet in our actual living still react, judge, and act by our natural self (Gal. 2:20).
- Receiving much but flowing out little: We continually hear messages, attend trainings, and share in meetings, yet seldom contact, supply, and shepherd people in our daily living (2 Cor. 4:12).
- Rich in the truth but limited in practice: We understand God’s ordained way, yet have no specific people in our prayer, gospel preaching, home meetings, and shepherding (Acts 20:20).
- Emphasizing doctrinal correctness but lacking the reality of love: We can point out others’ problems, yet may not cherish, nourish, bear with, and restore them (1 Cor. 8:1; 1 Thes. 2:7–12).
- Making plans for the work but lacking the Spirit’s present leading: We may be busy with activities and spreading, yet have little fellowship with the Lord or experience of being adjusted by Him (Acts 16:6–10).

What we most need to beware of is not that we do not know the truth, but that we assume knowing the truth means experiencing it, or that speaking the truth in a meeting means we have supplied Christ into people.

#### What is a genuinely open vessel?

Second Corinthians 4:7 says, “But we have this treasure in earthen vessels.” Galatians 2:20 says, “It is no longer I who live, but it is Christ who lives in me.” Thus, a genuine vessel has four inseparable aspects:

- Inwardly, receive the Lord: Continually allow Christ to enter, fill, saturate, and transform us.
- Outwardly, flow out the Lord: Not only share in meetings, but live out and supply Christ at home, at work, in society, in gospel contacts, in shepherding, and in coordination.
- Go forward by following the Lord: Not only wait passively, but actively pursue, receive the sending, announce the truth, bear fruit, perfect the saints, and participate in the Lord’s move on earth.
- Express the Lord together with all the saints: Not only be an open vessel individually, but have all the saints open together, supply one another, and coordinate in action (Eph. 4:15–16).

These are not four successive stages. They are the reality of one life lived at the same time in the individual, in our actions and relationships, and in the Body. An open vessel is not inactive; it acts without acting independently of the Lord. It is not merely filled with knowledge; it allows the truth to become the Christ we experience and live out!

### Paul as a balanced, all-round open vessel: eleven aspects advancing together

#### 1. A vessel containing the living Christ

Verses: Acts 9:15; 2 Cor. 4:7; Gal. 1:16; 2:20; 4:19; Phil. 1:20–21. The Lord chose Paul as His vessel not merely to possess rich knowledge concerning Christ, but to have Christ revealed in him, formed in him, and magnified from him. Paul testified, “For to me, to live is Christ” (Phil. 1:21). Burden: We should not merely know Christ but contain, experience, and live Christ, so that the Christ we speak of becomes the Christ others contact through us.

#### 2. A vessel receiving the Spirit

Verses: 2 Cor. 3:6, 17–18; Phil. 1:19; Rom. 8:4; Gal. 5:16, 25; Rev. 1:4; 4:5; 5:6. Paul knew that the Lord is the Spirit. He experienced the bountiful supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ, lived and walked by the Spirit, and was transformed as he beheld and reflected the Lord. Revelation further unveils the seven Spirits, the sevenfold intensified work of the Spirit for the carrying out of God’s economy. This is a further revelation concerning the Spirit in the New Testament, not a title directly used in Paul’s Epistles. Burden: We should not only accumulate the truth we have already understood; we should continually open to the living Lord and receive the Spirit’s fresh supply, enlightening, moving, and adjustment.

#### 3. A vessel passing through the cross and being transformed

Verses: 2 Cor. 1:9; 3:18; 4:10–12, 16; Phil. 3:10. Paul experienced affliction, breaking, being put to death, and renewal. He no longer trusted in himself but in God, who raises the dead. “So then death operates in us, but life in you” (2 Cor. 4:12). Burden: We should not only seek effective work or to be useful; we should let the cross deal with our natural man so that the resurrection life of Christ can truly supply others.

#### 4. A grain multiplying life

Verses: John 12:24; 1 Cor. 4:15; 10:17; Acts 13:49; 19:20. Christ is the grain of wheat that fell into the ground and died to produce many grains. We are the many grains produced by Him, multiplying life according to the same principle of death and resurrection. Through the gospel Paul begot spiritual children, not only causing people to be saved but also making them one loaf—the Body of Christ. Burden: We should not seek only personal growth in life; we should let Christ multiply from within us. We should not seek only an increase in numbers, but the blending of many grains into one Body.

#### 5. A branch of the vine bearing fruit

Verses: John 15:4–5, 8, 16; Col. 1:10; Rom. 1:13; Phil. 1:22. A branch abides in the vine, receives the rich supply of life, and spontaneously bears fruit. Message Seven of the 2026 Summer Training particularly emphasizes that in every aspect the branches need to live Christ, grow Christ, express Christ, and multiply Christ. Burden: Fruit-bearing is neither working apart from the Lord nor merely enjoying Him in meetings. It is allowing the life of Christ to flow out in our daily living, contacting people, preaching the gospel, and shepherding, to bear remaining fruit.

#### 6. A vessel one with the Lord in His move and sent by the Holy Spirit

Verses: Acts 13:1–4; 16:6–10; Rom. 15:18–19. The saints in Antioch served the Lord and fasted, and the Holy Spirit spoke and sent Barnabas and Paul. In Acts 16 Paul accepted both the Spirit’s forbidding and the call to Macedonia, and acted immediately. Burden: We should not first make our own plans and then ask the Lord to bless them. Rather, through prayer and fellowship in the Body, we should receive the Lord’s leading. When He says stop, stop; when He says go, go. Be one with the Lord in His move.

#### 7. A vessel honorable, useful, and equipped

Verses: 2 Tim. 2:2–6, 15, 20–22; 4:2. This is a point especially strengthened in Message Seven of the 2026 Summer Training. Second Timothy 2:21 says an honorable vessel is sanctified, useful to the Master, and prepared unto every good work. The ministry further points out that such honorable vessels are “preventers” of the church’s degradation and have five positive functions:

- **Teacher (2:2):** Commit the healthy words to faithful men who will be competent to teach others also, continuing the New Testament ministry.
- **Soldier (2:3–4):** Fight for the Lord’s interests, avoid entanglement with the affairs of this life, and refuse to compromise with dissenting opinions and different teachings.
- **Runner and contender (2:5):** Pursue and strive lawfully, not seeking results by natural means.
- **Farmer (2:6):** Labor patiently, sow, cultivate, and care for others, waiting for life to grow and bear fruit.
- **Workman (2:15):** Cut straight the word of the truth, neither distorting it nor being biased, and supply the truth accurately.

Second Timothy 2:15 should be connected with 4:2. A workman must not only cut straight the word of the truth; he must also announce it in season and out of season! “Proclaim the word; be ready in season and out of season.” This joins receiving, understanding, and rightly explaining the truth with actively announcing it. We cannot be merely collectors, students, and appreciators of the truth, or even only sharers in meetings. We must be living vessels who are equipped to teach, willing to fight, ready to run, patient in labor, able to cut straight the truth, and prepared to announce the Lord’s word at all times. This is not working apart from being open; it is the proper expression of a vessel filled by the Lord and useful to the Master.

#### 8. A vessel practicing God’s ordained way

Verses: Acts 14:21–23; 20:20, 31; 1 Cor. 4:15; 1 Thes. 2:7–12; 2 Tim. 2:2. Paul begot spiritual children through the gospel, cherished them as a nursing mother, exhorted them as a father, taught publicly and from house to house, perfected faithful ones, and established local churches. Acts 20:20 says, “I did not withhold any of those things that are profitable by not declaring them to you and by not teaching you publicly and from house to house.” Burden: We should not only preach God’s ordained way from a platform; we should enter people’s lives, beget, nourish, teach, and build them up. Through practicing together, all the saints can participate and carry it out.

#### 9. A vessel shepherding people long-term, with tears night and day

Verses: Acts 20:28–32; 1 Thes. 2:7–12; 2 Cor. 11:28–29; Col. 1:28–29. Paul not only spread the gospel actively; he also admonished the saints with tears night and day. He cherished them as a nursing mother, exhorted them as a father, and daily bore the concern of all the churches. His goal was “to present every man full-grown in Christ” (Col. 1:28). Burden: Spreading cannot replace shepherding, and numbers cannot replace growth to maturity in life. We must bring people into Christ and accompany, cherish, nourish, and perfect them until they can experience and follow the Lord and supply others themselves.

#### 10. A vessel for the building up of the Body, faithful to the end

Verses: Eph. 3:17–19; 4:11–16; 1 Cor. 12:12–27; Col. 2:19; 2 Tim. 4:7–8. Paul not only pursued living Christ personally; he also brought all the saints into the reality of the Body. He did not merely function himself but perfected all the members to function. Through hardship, imprisonment, and persecution, he remained faithful until he finished the course. Burden: We should not reproduce one individual’s ability but perfect all the members to exercise, coordinate, and function together. We should not establish a personal work system but build up the organic Body of Christ and prepare the bride to welcome the Lord’s return.

#### 11. An apostle producing and experiencing corporate open vessels

Verses: Acts 13:1–4; 16:6–10; 20:4; 1 Cor. 1:1; 2 Cor. 1:1; Gal. 1:2; Phil. 1:1; Eph. 4:16. Paul was not a great apostle who acted independently, alone, and only by his own spiritual ability. He was an open vessel, and through fellowship and coordination in the Body he also became one who produced corporate open vessels.

1. **Acts 13—Opening together to receive the Spirit’s speaking and sending.** Five prophets and teachers in the church in Antioch served the Lord and fasted together. The Spirit spoke, set apart Barnabas and Saul, and the saints fasted, prayed, laid their hands on them, and sent them away. The Spirit was the source of the sending, while fellowship in the Body supplied the coordination and confirmation. This was not one individual vessel with an external team added to it; the saints opened to the Spirit together, and the Lord’s move issued from among them.
2. **Acts 16—Discerning the Spirit’s leading together and moving together.** The Spirit forbade them, the Spirit of Jesus did not allow them, and then the vision of Macedonia came. Acts 16:10 says “we”; the group concluded that God had called them to announce the gospel and made arrangements to go. One person may receive a vision, but through fellowship the group can understand it together and coordinate with the Lord’s move. A personal revelation should not become a basis for acting independently.
3. **The joint greetings in eight Epistles—High revelation does not depart from fellowship in the Body.** Other brothers are listed as co-senders at the opening of eight Epistles. This does not prove that each one participated in writing them, but it clearly shows that Paul did not separate his apostolic ministry from fellowship with his co-workers. He willingly let the brothers greet and serve the churches together with him.
4. **Not merely leading co-workers but perfecting many vessels able to bear the commission.** In 2 Timothy 2:2 Paul charged Timothy, who in turn was to commit the things he had heard to faithful men able to teach others also. A genuine teacher does not maintain others’ dependence on him; he perfects more people to speak the truth, supply Christ, and continue perfecting others.
5. **Corporate openness is not an outward addition of organizations; it is receiving and living out Christ together.** A few people meeting or completing a project together does not automatically equal the reality of the Body. Corporate openness means that all receive the dispensing of the same Christ and supply, coordinate, and build up one another in one spirit: receiving together (Acts 13), moving together (Acts 16), and building together (Eph. 4:15–16).

Burden: Do not merely reproduce one capable leader. Produce companies of corporate vessels who open, exercise, live, spread, shepherd, and build together.

### Seven balances in Paul’s life that must not be neglected

These eleven aspects are not eleven successive stages to be completed one by one. We do not wait until we have received enough of Christ before bearing fruit, until we have matured enough before being sent, or until we have shepherded enough before participating in the building up. Rather, in our daily living, every meeting, and every service, all eleven aspects are exercised together, supply one another, strengthen one another, and continually advance.

1. **Truth and life:** Not only know the Bible and the ministry, but experience and live out the Christ revealed in the truth.
2. **Receiving and flowing out:** Not only be filled with Christ, but supply Christ in our living, gospel work, and shepherding.
3. **Enjoyment and multiplication:** Not only abide in the Lord, but actively bear fruit and bring in the increase of life.
4. **Waiting and acting:** Do not act by natural zeal; actively seek, obey, and follow the Spirit’s sending.
5. **Spreading and shepherding:** Not only gain new ones, but cherish, nourish, and teach them over the long term until they mature.
6. **The individual and the Body:** Not only live Christ and function ourselves, but perfect all the members to coordinate and build together.
7. **Zeal and faithfulness:** Not only be burning for a time, but pass through the transformation of the cross, labor patiently, and remain faithful to the end.

### Four crucial points

First, being open does not mean merely receiving knowledge. We are not vessels filled only with knowledge of the Bible and the ministry; we are vessels containing the living Christ, filled with the Spirit, and allowing Christ to become our practical living.

Second, flowing out does not mean merely sharing in meetings. The genuine flow is Christ living out from within us to reach our family, work, friends, gospel contacts, and the saints, becoming the multiplication of life and genuine shepherding.

Third, being open does not mean taking no positive action. Paul was a teacher, soldier, runner, farmer, and workman. He received the sending, cut straight the word of the truth, and announced the Lord’s word in season and out of season. He labored greatly, but his labor issued from grace and was one with the Spirit.

Fourth, individual openness must lead to the building up together. What God gains should not be only a few especially spiritual or capable vessels, but many living vessels who are open to one another, exercise together, coordinate, and continually multiply, bringing forth the organic building up of the Body of Christ.

### Conclusion

An open vessel does not only receive without flowing out, enjoy without bearing fruit, understand without practicing, wait without acting, spread without shepherding, or function individually without caring for the Body. Rather, we open to the Lord daily and are filled, transformed, and constituted with Him. We are one with Him in our living and actions, actively announcing, multiplying, shepherding, and perfecting, and building up the Body of Christ with all the members to accomplish God’s eternal economy!
